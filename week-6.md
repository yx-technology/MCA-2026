[Verovio - Because I Love You (Yoo Jae-Ha) with Metadata v2](verovio3.html)

Last week, I already included the bibliography section to include the [`<genre>`](https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/v5/elements/genre.html)  element as part of the Metadata. This week, this move proved to be a right decision as it enabled me to add on the [`<availability>`](https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/v5/elements/availability.html)  section without editing my entire Metadata, confirming that by properly structuring and using specific elements, any future edits would be much easier.

The instructions hinted at using the [`<notesStmt>`](https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/v5/elements/notesStmt.html)  to record the genre of this piece of music. However, after evaluating the overall need to record the Genre Classification and the Specific Licensing Data, I made the choice to keep using the element [`<genre>`](https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/v5/elements/genre.html) and simply added the [`<availability>`](https://music-encoding.org/guidelines/v5/elements/availability.html) element as it saved time while still achieving the goal of recording the Metadata.  

The decision of which Creative Commons license to include for my document, I searched through the [Creative Commons Search Portal](https://search.creativecommons.org/) to check the existing  licensing of the original published song. My only lead was on [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6O8oM-vPRtY) and there was one licensing that looked like this ℗. Further research shows that this sign means [Phonographic Copyright](https://copyrightservice.co.uk/copyright/copyright_symbol). In sum, there are 2 specific license that one could attach to their music, first being the Music Composition Line ©, and second being the Song Recording Line ℗. In this case, ℗ meant that specifically, the original/main audio recording is protected under copyright. The Creative Commons (CC) licensing then sits on top of these specific licenses where both ℗ and CC are applicable to the piece of curated music.  

Layer 1: Original song copyright -> [ ℗ ]  
Layer 2: Creative Commons copyright of this curated music -> [ Attribution ShareAlike (CC BY-SA) ]  

<ins>Reflection</ins>  
After going through this process, I realised that licensing puts out a guideline for users like myself in trying to encode music. This allows me to clearly understand how the publisher intends for their work to be used breaching any legal boundaries. However, our in-class disucssions revealed to me that licensing has many grey areas. Since music specifically is a sound vibration, copyright would be harder to enforce especially since there are many sources of music that we can turn to. Therefore, as an encoder or even as a musician, it is helpful to include educate ourselves on copyrights  to ensure that we do our best to honour the original artiste, composers, publishers etc. and to protect ourselves from potential lawsuits.
