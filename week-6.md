<ins>Reflection</ins>  
Last week, I already included the bibliography section to include the genre element as part of the Metadata. This week, this move proved to be a right decision as it enabled me to add on the "availability" section without editing my entire Metadata, confirming that my comments on future editing of Metadata is easier when the proper structure is utilised.  

The instructions hinted at using the "Notes statement" to record the genre of this piece of music. However, after evaluating the overall need to record the Genre Classification and the Specific Licensing Data, I made the choice to keep using the element "genre" and simply added the "availability" element as it saved time while still achieving the goal of recording the Metadata.  

The decision of which Creative Commons license to include for my document, I searched through the Creative Commons Search Portal to check the existing  licensing of the original published song. My only lead was on YouTube and there was one licensing that looked like this ℗. Further research shows that this sign means Phonogram. There are 2 specific license that one could attach to their music, first being the Music Composition Line ©, and second being the Song Recording Line ℗. In this case, the ℗ meant that the original/main audio recording is protected under copyright. The Creative Commons (CC) licensing then sits on top of these specific licenses where both ℗ and CC are applicable to the curated music.  

Visually, this looks like this:
Layer 1: Original song copyright -> [ ℗ ] 
Layer 2: Creative Commons copyright of this curated music -> [ Attribution ShareAlike (CC BY-SA) ]
