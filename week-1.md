#### Task 2:  
For the majority of my life, learning how to play an instrument (eg. piano) comes at a cost. Piano classes, piano scores, buying a piano etc. On top of that, most people around me are motivated to play the piano only because its a great talent that parents want their child to aquire and also a status symbol based of one's upbringing. With technology, now people can learn to play a piece without buying the scores. These are made free online. If one has exceptional talent, they can even arrange a piece by listening to the original song and create a piano version. Of course, this could potentially lead to less of a preference for live music and musicians might give up playing the instrument at all since technology can mimick the sounds of a piano. Music is meant to be appreciated and I think technology has helped bring artistes closer to their audiences regardless of where they are. It has also opened up access to the high barrier of entry to learning the piano.

Abit of background: this song was part of the only solo album Yoo released before his passing, where it was initially passed off as a strange melody at the time but later celebrated as a classic Korean ballad and touted as the beginning of the genre - Korean ballads. This song is available on many streaming platforms and on YouTube, however, it was originally released as a vinyl, an indication of the history of this song. Although my first interaction with this song was a piano composition played by George Harliono, hearing the version with the lyrics gave meaning to this piece beyond the musical composition. As a non-korean myself, I also understand that some of the lyrical meaning could be lost in translation despite technological advancements in translation. Because of its rise to fame many years after its release, there are many covers and versions of the songs in different forms such as piano compositions or guitar versions. It was later rereleased on casette and on CDs as well, creating a range of archives of the music. Personally, I believe that the music will sound different in its different forms. Yoo played the instruments himself, and despite the many years after his death, the song lives on thanks to preservation efforts fueled by the love for this song. I do wonder if his song did not become as widely accepted by the public, what it would become right now. Would I still have been able to hear this song and appreciate its melody thousands of miles away from where the original physical recording? Would this music be buried somewhere and left to die away with the dependence on physical records? Will the quality of the sound and music nuances change as the copies are repeatedly used? Do the digital recordings reflect the music Yoo wanted to present? I appreciate that there are musical preservation methods nowadays but I also believe that perhaps it is fueled by capitalism, where the recording studios focus on songs that people love and that could generate more profit than pushing the creative songs out to people. This could be a systematic problem and perhaps thats why independent artistes struggle even till today.
